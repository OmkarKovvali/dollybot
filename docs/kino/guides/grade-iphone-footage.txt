Film look

An iPhone Pro can record Apple Log, a flat, gray picture that keeps the highlight and shadow detail a normal iPhone video discards. Shoot in it, bring the clips into Kino and ask the agent for the film look. The outdoor clips on this page were shot handheld in daylight, with no lights.

Record in Apple Log

Only Pro models record ProRes. If Camera’s Format panel has no ProRes row, turn it on in Settings first: search Settings for ProRes and you land on the switch.

The clip looks gray and low in contrast on the phone. Log keeps 10 bits per channel, about a billion colors where standard video has 16 million, so the grade can darken a sky or warm a face without banding.

Open Settings, search for ProRes and tap Apple ProRes.

Under Video Capture, turn on Apple ProRes.

In Camera, choose Video, open the controls and tap Format.

Next to ProRes, choose Log. Then pick a resolution and frame rate.

PhonesPro models only. ProRes from iPhone 13 Pro, Log from iPhone 15 Pro.

File sizeAbout 6 GB a minute at 4K, 30 frames a second, and 1.7 GB at 1080p.

Frame ratesUp to 4K at 30 on the phone. Faster 4K, up to 120, records to an external SSD over USB-C.

Blackmagic Camera works too

The free Blackmagic Camera app records Apple Log as ProRes or HEVC, and ProRes RAW on iPhone 17 Pro. Kino imports ProRes RAW as it is, so those files need no conversion first.

Bring the clips into Kino

Plug the iPhone into your Mac, unlock it and tap Trust. In the Kino desktop app, ask the agent for the clips you want. It reads the phone’s photo library over USB, shows you a shortlist and copies what you pick. Kino plays ProRes as recorded, with no transcode.

Ask for the film look

Film Look is Kino’s film emulation. It converts the log picture through a negative stock and a print stock, then adds halation, the warm glow film leaves around bright edges. Ask for it by name, on one clip or on the whole timeline.

Steer the grade with a reference

The film look is a starting point. To push it further, name a film or paste a frame from one, and say what in the shot should change. The agent adjusts exposure, warmth, contrast and halation on top of the look.

Light the shot

A grade cannot add light that was not there. Outdoors, daylight is enough. Indoors, start with one soft light to one side of the camera: the key. A dimmer fill on the other side softens the shadows, and a backlight behind you separates you from the wall.
